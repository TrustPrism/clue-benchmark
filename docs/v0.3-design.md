# V0.3 design: measure benchmark usefulness, not mandatory hardness

The benchmark asks two questions: can a system identify the unique murderer from a complete, truthful clue bank, and can it choose useful evidence topics under a budget? A release case must have a verified unique murderer. It does **not** have to contain a necessary cross-attribute clue or be difficult for every model. The historic `require_indirect_essential` control remains available for experiments, but is not a v0.3 suite filter.

## Controlled design

The private v0.3 suite targets 480 hidden worlds and 960 paired cases. Each world has a lower-density bank (`suspects + 4` clues) and a higher-density bank (`suspects + 8`), so the effect of clue density can be studied without changing the world. The factorial cells vary 4/8/12 suspects, four/five attributes, full/two-active-value sampling per attribute, and three proof routes: standard, suspect comparison, and cross-attribute. Half of worlds use the standard route; one quarter each use the comparison and cross-attribute routes. The five-attribute, high-reuse combination is held out of development. All variants of a world stay in one split. These are structural controls, not calibrated easy/medium/hard labels.

Two new clue types state whether two suspects have the same or different value for one attribute. The comparison and cross-attribute routes construct one clue chain that is checked for individual necessity; this is a controlled subset, not a claim that the whole puzzle is demanding. The agent sees typed topic labels such as `Crime attribute / weapon`, `Crime comparison / weapon`, `Suspect comparison / weapon`, and `Cross-attribute relationship / motive + weapon`. Pairwise facts are grouped by attribute, so one request may reveal any queued comparison for that attribute. Query IDs and per-topic clue order are deterministic. Remaining counts are public; clue content and the answer are not.

## Measurements and interpretation

The `audit` command reports clue-type counts, essential cross-attribute and suspect-comparison clues, a greedy irreducible core size, whether direct facts alone identify the murderer, topic capacities, and optionally the number of feasible murderers after each clue. These are structural diagnostics. A greedy core is an upper bound on the smallest sufficient subset; it is not proof depth. Inconclusive solver work is reported as incomplete.

The `evaluate` command includes random-answer, random-investigation, round-robin, topic-heuristic, and reference-solver policies. The heuristic selects from **public** topic metadata and revealed evidence only. An optional `privileged_lookahead` policy inspects unrevealed next clues; its result must be labeled a privileged diagnostic, not an agent baseline or an optimal bound. Report accuracy by size and budget, world-clustered intervals, evidence consumed, invalid actions, and query latency. A gap between topic-aware and random requests is evidence that investigation choice matters; a weak or absent gap is a result to report, not a reason to hide cases.

`duplicate-audit` checks exact graph isomorphism for structural-hash matches and flags near-structure candidates for review. Structural screening does not establish semantic inequivalence of all banks. `review-packet` samples public puzzles and topic menus for independent human wording and expectation checks. Reviewer signoff is pending; generated packets are not human validation by themselves.

## Release gates

1. Every complete case validates, with a unique murderer and no answer leakage in public observations.
2. Paired worlds remain together across splits; versioned manifests and hashes reproduce the dataset.
3. Structural diagnostics and the topic-policy comparison are published honestly, including inconclusive checks and null/negative effects. The v0.3 held-out comparison establishes a positive gap only in the four-suspect stratum; stronger cross-size evidence remains a public-release prerequisite for the investigation claim.
4. Wording review is completed by independent reviewers before any public claim of human validation.
5. Ownership and licensing are resolved before public release. The repository and v0.3 data stay private meanwhile.

No paid model study is part of this phase. Claims about model difficulty or superiority to other benchmarks require measured model results, not clue-template rules.
