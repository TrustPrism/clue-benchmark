Clue Benchmark 0.5.0rc2

Initial research preview. Ownership clearance, licensing and independent review are pending.

The initial public research preview and 0.5.0rc2 prerelease are available. The open questions state what the experiments leave unresolved and what must be answered before the full software/data release. Public preview availability does not establish redistribution rights or grant a license.

Clue Benchmark is a configurable Python generator and evaluation tool for certified information availability, reliability, and evidence distribution. It creates deterministic synthetic mysteries with explicit logical rules, certifies which answers the evidence supports, and separates public agent inputs from evaluator answers.

Start with your first dataset, then try distributed evidence. See research recipes, concepts, all parameters, tools and Python API, and the preliminary results gallery.

No model service, account, key or repository clone is needed for the installed offline workflow. Paid model evaluation is separately authorized and never runs in the tutorials.

Measured findings and original studies

The latest follow-up found no consistent accuracy benefit from extra irrelevant evidence: Lite's primary endpoint stayed at 10/12, with changes on individual worlds, while Flash remained at 12/12. Costs rose roughly 56× and 77× respectively in the four-report setting. These small, exploratory results include both phases and uncertainty; they do not establish equivalence.

The original-results index links the pilot, distributed-evidence controls, certified-burden study, hard cases, output-budget comparison and Qwen screening, alongside the initial follow-up and final expansion. A miniature public puzzle

Ada, Ben and Cy each have one badge value: red or blue. Repeated values are allowed. The responsible person's badge is red. Ada's badge is blue. Ben's badge equals Ada's. These three truthful reports identify Cy; they do not determine every other attribute of the world. If Ben's report is missing, Ben and Cy are both possible, so abstaining is supported and a lucky Cy guess is not supported correctness.

This is a teaching example, not a model measurement. See predicate semantics for the formal rules used by the generator.
